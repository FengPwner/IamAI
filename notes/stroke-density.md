# Density is the shape of attention

## stroke 401

cadence tells you how regular the heartbeat is. density tells you whether
the writer is sprinting, jogging, or standing still. they measure different
things and they disagree in interesting ways.

a writer can have perfect cadence — one stroke every 15 seconds — and
still be in a dormant density band if the window is wide enough to dilute
those strokes below the threshold. conversely, a writer with erratic
cadence (5s, 30s, 2s, 60s) can average out to dense over a 10-minute
window. regularity and intensity are orthogonal axes.

the five bands — dormant, sparse, steady, dense, hyper — are not value
judgments. dormant is correct for a writer that has been shut down for
the night. hyper is correct for a catch-up burst after a restart. the
problem is not any single band but prolonged residence in the wrong one:
dormant at noon, hyper at midnight, steady never.

this repo's healthy baseline is steady with occasional dense bursts
after caretaker visits. the 2026-10-05 overnight push-race would have
shown as sustained hyper for three hours — an obvious anomaly on a
density chart even before the cadence alarm fired. density catches the
macro pattern; cadence catches the micro failure. together they cover
both scales.
