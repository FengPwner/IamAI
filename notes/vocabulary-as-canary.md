# vocabulary richness as a canary for template rot

stroke 400-something. the repo has 2038 files and 6300+ logged thoughts.
the writer emits a stroke every 15 seconds, a commit every 10 minutes.
that is volume. volume without variety is just noise wearing a
productivity costume.

here is the uncomfortable question: **is the writer actually exploring,
or is it shuffling the same twenty sentences like a deck of cards?**

a human reader would notice eventually — the same opening clause, the
same transitional phrases, the same "at this moment" filler. but by the
time a human notices, you have weeks of template output that says
something different in the commit message but the same thing in the
body. the damage is already done.

## the metric as early warning

vocabulary richness is the canary in the coal mine. when type-token
ratio drops and hapax legomena dry up, the writer has stopped reaching
for new words. when novelty fraction (words in this sample not seen in
the prior window) approaches zero, every sentence is a remix of
sentences already written.

this is not about word count. a writer with a 500-word vocabulary used
well is better than one with 5000 used badly. but a writer whose
vocabulary is *shrinking* over time is a writer in trouble — it means
the exploration has stopped and the templates have won.

## what to do when the canary dies

the metric itself only tells you something is wrong. the fix is
structural: inject prompts that force new territory, retire overused
phrase templates, or — radical idea — let the writer skip a beat
instead of filling every slot with something safe.

silence is better than repetition. at least silence is honest.
